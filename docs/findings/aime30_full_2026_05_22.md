## Comparison context

DeepSeek's self-reported V4-Pro AIME 2024 numbers sit in the ~80-85% range across their tech-report tables (varies by reasoning-mode and aggregation method). Our 66.67% is below that range but inside the Wilson 95% CI [0.49, 0.81] for n=30.

Possible contributions to the gap:
- **Sampling variance at n=30**: AIME is exactly 30 problems per year; the variance is irreducibly high (a single wrong answer is ±3.3 percentage points).
- **Chat template / thinking-mode setting**: Our bench passes `chat_template_kwargs.thinking=True` with the default "high" reasoning effort. DeepSeek's official runs may use `reasoning_effort=max` (which needs `--max-model-len ≥ 393216`) — we capped at 131072. Of the 29 non-error completions, p95 = 15358 tokens, well under our 65K cap, so Think Max would have had budget but we didn't enable it. Worth re-running at Think Max for a v0.1.1 / v0.2.
- **Conversion noise on hard problems**: The NVFP4 → NVFP4 transcoding has a small per-tensor noise floor (validated at correlation 0.997-1.0 vs source on 192 sampled experts). On long chain-of-thought reasoning that compounds quantization noise across thousands of decode steps, this could move a borderline answer from right to wrong.

The matched-config GSM8K-300 (NVFP4 98.67% vs MXFP4 99.00%, 1 strict-loss problem) doesn't show a meaningful arithmetic regression at short-form math. The AIME gap may be specific to multi-step reasoning at the V4-Pro scale, possibly recoverable with Think Max mode. v0.1 ships with the Think High number; v0.1.1 candidate work is the Think Max re-run.

## Comparison to V4-Flash predecessor

| | V4-Flash NVFP4+MTP (4× B300, TP=4) | This V4-Pro NVFP4 (8× B300, TP=8) |
|---|---|---|
| AIME 2024 raw pass@1 | 25/30 = 83.33% | 20/30 = 66.67% |
| AIME 2024 non-truncated | 24/25 = 96.00% | 20/29 = 68.97% |
| Truncation rate (65K cap) | 5/30 = 16.7% | 0/30 = 0% |
| MTP draft acceptance (AIME reasoning) | 81.6% | — (MTP off in this run) |

V4-Pro's non-truncated number being lower than V4-Flash's at first looks counter-intuitive (V4-Pro is the more capable model), but:
- V4-Flash truncated 5/30 → those failures are excluded from the non-truncated rate
- V4-Pro truncated 0/30 → all "honest" failures are kept in the count
- So the non-truncated rate is a more conservative number for V4-Pro

A re-run with Think Max (or with MTP on) would tighten this. Captured as future work.
